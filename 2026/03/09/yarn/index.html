Transformers have no built-in notion of order. Feed the same set of tokens in any permutation and the attention scores are unchanged — making two sequences identical from the model’s perspective even if their meanings are entirely different. Positional encoding patches this gap: it injects sequence position into each token’s representation so that the model can tell “the cat sat on the mat” apart from “the mat sat on the cat.”

This post traces the evolution of positional encoding from the original sinusoidal scheme through Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) and its long-context extensions — Position Interpolation (PI), NTK-aware scaling, and finally YaRN — deriving each step carefully along the way.

1. Sinusoidal Positional Encoding

Ignoring multi-head structure, the projected query matrix Q∈RS×DH\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D_H}Q∈RS×DH​ is a row-stack of per-token vectors:

Q=[q0Tq1T⋮qS−1T]\mathbf{Q} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{q}_0^{\mathsf{T}} \\ \mathbf{q}_1^{\mathsf{T}} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{q}_{S-1}^{\mathsf{T}} \end{bmatrix} Q=​q0T​q1T​⋮qS−1T​​​

where qm∈RDH\mathbf{q}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{D_H}qm​∈RDH​ is the query vector for the token at position mmm. The simplest positional fix is additive: replace qm\mathbf{q}_mqm​ with qm+pm\mathbf{q}_m + \mathbf{p}_mqm​+pm​, where pm\mathbf{p}_mpm​ depends only on mmm.

Frequency vector. Define DH/2D_H/2DH​/2 angular frequencies, one per dimension pair jjj:

ωj=1θ2j/DH,j=0,1,…,DH2−1\omega_j = \frac{1}{\theta^{2j/D_H}}, \quad j = 0, 1, \ldots, \frac{D_H}{2}-1 ωj​=θ2j/DH​1​,j=0,1,…,2DH​​−1

with base θ=10,000\theta = 10{,}000θ=10,000. The highest frequency is ω0=1\omega_0 = 1ω0​=1 and the frequencies decay geometrically: ωDH/2−1=θ−(DH−2)/DH≈1/θ\omega_{D_H/2-1} = \theta^{-(D_H-2)/D_H} \approx 1/\thetaωDH​/2−1​=θ−(DH​−2)/DH​≈1/θ.

Position encoding vector. For the token at position mmm:

pm=[sin⁡(mω0),  cos⁡(mω0),  sin⁡(mω1),  cos⁡(mω1),  …,  sin⁡(mωDH/2−1),  cos⁡(mωDH/2−1)]T\mathbf{p}_m = \bigl[\sin(m\omega_0),\;\cos(m\omega_0),\;\sin(m\omega_1),\;\cos(m\omega_1),\;\ldots,\;\sin(m\omega_{D_H/2-1}),\;\cos(m\omega_{D_H/2-1})\bigr]^{\mathsf{T}} pm​=[sin(mω0​),cos(mω0​),sin(mω1​),cos(mω1​),…,sin(mωDH​/2−1​),cos(mωDH​/2−1​)]T

Each position gets a unique fingerprint and nearby positions remain close in encoding space. The main limitation is that the encoding is absolute: the model sees positions mmm and nnn but must infer their relative gap n−mn - mn−m from the encoded values. Rotary encoding resolves this algebraically.

2. Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)

Instead of adding a position vector, RoPE rotates query and key vectors in 2D subspaces before computing attention. The encoding of relative distance is then exact and guaranteed by the structure of the rotation group.

2.1 The Rotation Matrix

For the token at position mmm, define the block-diagonal rotation matrix Rm∈RDH×DH\mathbf{R}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{D_H \times D_H}Rm​∈RDH​×DH​:

Rm=[cos⁡(mω0)−sin⁡(mω0)00⋯00sin⁡(mω0)cos⁡(mω0)00⋯0000cos⁡(mω1)−sin⁡(mω1)⋯0000sin⁡(mω1)cos⁡(mω1)⋯00⋮⋮⋮⋮⋱⋮⋮0000⋯cos⁡(mωDH/2−1)−sin⁡(mωDH/2−1)0000⋯sin⁡(mωDH/2−1)cos⁡(mωDH/2−1)]\mathbf{R}_m = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(m\omega_0) & -\sin(m\omega_0) & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ \sin(m\omega_0) & \cos(m\omega_0) & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \cos(m\omega_1) & -\sin(m\omega_1) & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \sin(m\omega_1) & \cos(m\omega_1) & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \cos(m\omega_{D_H/2-1}) & -\sin(m\omega_{D_H/2-1}) \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \sin(m\omega_{D_H/2-1}) & \cos(m\omega_{D_H/2-1}) \end{bmatrix} Rm​=​cos(mω0​)sin(mω0​)00⋮00​−sin(mω0​)cos(mω0​)00⋮00​00cos(mω1​)sin(mω1​)⋮00​00−sin(mω1​)cos(mω1​)⋮00​⋯⋯⋯⋯⋱⋯⋯​0000⋮cos(mωDH​/2−1​)sin(mωDH​/2−1​)​0000⋮−sin(mωDH​/2−1​)cos(mωDH​/2−1​)​​

There are DH/2D_H/2DH​/2 blocks; the jjj-th block is a 2D rotation by angle mωjm\omega_jmωj​. RoPE applies this matrix to each token’s query and key before attention:

q~m=Rmqm,k~m=Rmkm\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_m = \mathbf{R}_m \mathbf{q}_m, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_m = \mathbf{R}_m \mathbf{k}_m q~​m​=Rm​qm​,k~m​=Rm​km​

Since Rm\mathbf{R}_mRm​ is orthogonal, RmTRm=I\mathbf{R}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_m = \mathbf{I}RmT​Rm​=I. The composition law gives:

RmTRn=Rn−m\mathbf{R}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_n = \mathbf{R}_{n-m} RmT​Rn​=Rn−m​

because each jjj-th block satisfies R(−mωj) R(nωj)=R((n−m)ωj)R(-m\omega_j)\,R(n\omega_j) = R((n-m)\omega_j)R(−mωj​)R(nωj​)=R((n−m)ωj​).

2.2 Relative Distance Through Rotation

The attention score between query at position mmm and key at position nnn (ignoring the 1/DH1/\sqrt{D_H}1/DH​​ scale) is:

q~mTk~n=qmTRmTRnkn=qmTRn−mkn\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_n = \mathbf{q}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_n \mathbf{k}_n = \mathbf{q}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{n-m} \mathbf{k}_n q~​mT​k~n​=qmT​RmT​Rn​kn​=qmT​Rn−m​kn​

The absolute positions mmm and nnn cancel; only the relative offset n−mn - mn−m remains. The full attention matrix is:

Amn=qmTRn−m kn\mathbf{A}_{mn} = \mathbf{q}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{n-m}\, \mathbf{k}_n Amn​=qmT​Rn−m​kn​

Written out explicitly:

A=[q0Tk0q0TR1k1⋯q0TRS−1kS−1q1TR−1k0q1Tk1⋯q1TRS−2kS−1⋮⋮⋱⋮qS−1TR1−Sk0qS−1TR2−Sk1⋯qS−1TkS−1]\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{q}_0^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{k}_0 & \mathbf{q}_0^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{1} \mathbf{k}_1 & \cdots & \mathbf{q}_0^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{S-1} \mathbf{k}_{S-1} \\ \mathbf{q}_1^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{-1} \mathbf{k}_0 & \mathbf{q}_1^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{k}_1 & \cdots & \mathbf{q}_1^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{S-2} \mathbf{k}_{S-1} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \mathbf{q}_{S-1}^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{1-S} \mathbf{k}_0 & \mathbf{q}_{S-1}^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{2-S} \mathbf{k}_1 & \cdots & \mathbf{q}_{S-1}^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{k}_{S-1} \end{bmatrix} A=​q0T​k0​q1T​R−1​k0​⋮qS−1T​R1−S​k0​​q0T​R1​k1​q1T​k1​⋮qS−1T​R2−S​k1​​⋯⋯⋱⋯​q0T​RS−1​kS−1​q1T​RS−2​kS−1​⋮qS−1T​kS−1​​​

Each entry is determined purely by the token content vectors and the relative displacement. This is an exact algebraic identity, not an approximation, and it generalizes cleanly to any (m,n)(m, n)(m,n) pair seen during or after training.

3. Naive Extrapolation and Its Limits

3.1 The Frequency Grid

The angle applied to dimension pair jjj at position mmm is mωjm\omega_jmωj​. The full training grid is:

dim pair jjj m=0m=0m=0 m=1m=1m=1 ⋯\cdots⋯ m=S−1m=S-1m=S−1
j=0j=0j=0 000 ω0\omega_0ω0​ ⋯\cdots⋯ (S−1)ω0(S-1)\omega_0(S−1)ω0​
j=1j=1j=1 000 ω1\omega_1ω1​ ⋯\cdots⋯ (S−1)ω1(S-1)\omega_1(S−1)ω1​
⋮\vdots⋮ ⋮\vdots⋮ ⋮\vdots⋮ ⋱\ddots⋱ ⋮\vdots⋮
j=DH/2−1j=D_H/2-1j=DH​/2−1 000 ωDH/2−1\omega_{D_H/2-1}ωDH​/2−1​ ⋯\cdots⋯ (S−1)ωDH/2−1(S-1)\omega_{D_H/2-1}(S−1)ωDH​/2−1​

Naive extrapolation simply extends this table rightward: for positions m≥Sm \ge Sm≥S, continue with the same frequencies and compute angle mωjm\omega_jmωj​ as usual.

3.2 High-Frequency vs. Low-Frequency Dimensions

The wavelength (period for one full 2π2\pi2π rotation) of dimension pair jjj is:

Tj=2πωj=2π θ2j/DHT_j = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_j} = 2\pi\,\theta^{2j/D_H} Tj​=ωj​2π​=2πθ2j/DH​

  • High-frequency dimensions (jjj small, ωj\omega_jωj​ large): TjT_jTj​ is short — the angle mωjm\omega_jmωj​ completes many full revolutions within the training window [0,S−1][0,S-1][0,S−1]. Extrapolating to m>Sm>Sm>S merely continues a fast oscillation the model has observed in all its phases. These dimensions degrade gracefully under extrapolation.

  • Low-frequency dimensions (jjj large, ωj\omega_jωj​ small): TjT_jTj​ can greatly exceed SSS — the angle mωjm\omega_jmωj​ may not complete even one full revolution during training. Extending the sequence to S′>SS'>SS′>S exposes these dimensions to angles they have never seen at training time (out-of-distribution, OOD), causing the attention distribution to collapse and generation quality to sharply degrade.

Naive extrapolation assumes the model generalizes beyond SSS for all frequencies uniformly. It does for high-frequency dimensions; it fails for low-frequency ones.

4. Position Interpolation (PI)

4.1 The Linear Rescaling

Position Interpolation (Chen et al., 2023) takes the opposite approach: compress the target position range [0,S′−1][0, S'-1][0,S′−1] back into the training range [0,S−1][0, S-1][0,S−1] by scaling every position by S/S′S/S'S/S′:

m  ⟼  m′=m⋅SS′m \;\longmapsto\; m' = m \cdot \frac{S}{S'} m⟼m′=m⋅S′S​

The angle in dimension jjj at position mmm becomes:

m′ ωj=m⋅SS′⋅ωjm'\,\omega_j = m \cdot \frac{S}{S'} \cdot \omega_j m′ωj​=m⋅S′S​⋅ωj​

No angle ever exceeds the training-time maximum: the OOD problem disappears.

4.2 The Cost: Resolution Loss

Compression creates a new problem for high-frequency dimensions. Before interpolation, adjacent tokens (offset 1) produce an angle difference of ωj\omega_jωj​. After interpolation the same pair produces only ωj/s\omega_j / sωj​/s where s=S′/S>1s = S'/S > 1s=S′/S>1. For high-frequency dimensions, which rely on fine angular granularity to distinguish nearby tokens, this sss-fold reduction degrades local position resolution. Fine-tuning on longer sequences is typically required to recover performance.

In short: PI trades OOD degradation for resolution loss, and both problems arise from treating all frequency dimensions identically.

5. NTK-aware Scaling

5.1 The Base-Scaling Idea

NTK-aware RoPE (bloc97, 2023) addresses the high/low-frequency asymmetry with a single parameter change: replace the base θ\thetaθ with a scaled base αθ\alpha\thetaαθ, leaving position indices mmm unchanged.

Under the new base the frequency for dimension pair jjj becomes:

ωjnew=(αθ)−2j/DH=α−2j/DH⋅ωj\omega_j^{\text{new}} = (\alpha\theta)^{-2j/D_H} = \alpha^{-2j/D_H}\cdot\omega_j ωjnew​=(αθ)−2j/DH​=α−2j/DH​⋅ωj​

The angle at position mmm in dimension jjj is now m⋅α−2j/DH⋅ωjm\cdot\alpha^{-2j/D_H}\cdot\omega_jm⋅α−2j/DH​⋅ωj​, which is equivalent to keeping frequency ωj\omega_jωj​ but using a dimension-dependent effective position:

meff(j)=m⋅α−2j/DHm_{\text{eff}}(j) = m\cdot\alpha^{-2j/D_H} meff​(j)=m⋅α−2j/DH​

This per-dimension scale is far from uniform:

  • j=0j = 0j=0 (highest frequency): meff=m⋅α0=mm_{\text{eff}} = m\cdot\alpha^0 = mmeff​=m⋅α0=m — the position is unchanged. High-frequency dimensions remain in pure extrapolation mode, preserving local resolution.
  • j=DH/2−1j = D_H/2 - 1j=DH​/2−1 (lowest frequency): meff=m⋅α−(DH−2)/DHm_{\text{eff}} = m\cdot\alpha^{-(D_H-2)/D_H}meff​=m⋅α−(DH​−2)/DH​ — the position is compressed.

5.2 Choosing α\alphaα

We want the lowest-frequency dimension to mimic PI: the maximum position S′−1S'-1S′−1 should map to an effective position no larger than S−1S-1S−1. Setting:

α(DH−2)/DH=s=S′S\alpha^{(D_H-2)/D_H} = s = \frac{S'}{S} α(DH​−2)/DH​=s=SS′​

and solving:

α=sDH/(DH−2)\boxed{\alpha = s^{D_H/(D_H-2)}} α=sDH​/(DH​−2)​

With this choice the per-dimension position scales are:

Dimension pair jjj Effective scale α−2j/DH\alpha^{-2j/D_H}α−2j/DH​ Behavior
j=0j = 0j=0 111 Pure extrapolation — full resolution preserved
0<j<DH/2−10 < j < D_H/2-10<j<DH​/2−1 s−2j/(DH−2)s^{-2j/(D_H-2)}s−2j/(DH​−2) Intermediate compression
j=DH/2−1j = D_H/2-1j=DH​/2−1 s−1s^{-1}s−1 Full PI-style compression

NTK-aware provides a smooth spectrum from pure extrapolation (high frequency) to full position compression (low frequency), all from one scalar α\alphaα.

5.3 Equivalence of the Two Views

The per-dimension position scaling interpretation and the base-scaling implementation are algebraically identical. The frequency formula factors as:

1(αθ)2j/DH=1θ2j/DH⏟ωj⋅α−2j/DH\frac{1}{(\alpha\theta)^{2j/D_H}} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{\theta^{2j/D_H}}}_{\omega_j} \cdot \alpha^{-2j/D_H} (αθ)2j/DH​1​=ωj​θ2j/DH​1​​​⋅α−2j/DH​

The second factor multiplies the position mmm in the angle mωjm\omega_jmωj​, acting as exactly the dimension-dependent position scale derived in §5.2. There is no approximation: the two views are the same calculation written differently.

To see that the boundary condition forces α=sDH/(DH−2)\alpha = s^{D_H/(D_H-2)}α=sDH​/(DH​−2), note that the per-dimension scale g(j)=α−2j/DHg(j) = \alpha^{-2j/D_H}g(j)=α−2j/DH​ must satisfy:

g(0)=1✓g ⁣(DH2−1)=α−(DH−2)/DH=!s−1  ⟹  α(DH−2)/DH=s  ⟹  α=sDH/(DH−2)✓\begin{align*} g(0) &= 1 \quad\checkmark \\ g\!\left(\tfrac{D_H}{2}-1\right) &= \alpha^{-(D_H-2)/D_H} \stackrel{!}{=} s^{-1} \;\Longrightarrow\; \alpha^{(D_H-2)/D_H} = s \;\Longrightarrow\; \alpha = s^{D_H/(D_H-2)} \quad\checkmark \end{align*} g(0)g(2DH​​−1)​=1✓=α−(DH​−2)/DH​=!s−1⟹α(DH​−2)/DH​=s⟹α=sDH​/(DH​−2)✓​

6. YaRN: NTK-by-Parts

YaRN (Peng et al., 2023 — Yet Another RoPE extensioN method) refines NTK-aware by replacing its single global exponential curve with a piecewise strategy: partition all dimension pairs into three frequency bands and assign each the most appropriate treatment.

6.1 A Unified Interpolation Formula

YaRN parameterizes the per-dimension position scale h(j)∈[s−1, 1]h(j) \in [s^{-1},\,1]h(j)∈[s−1,1] through a blending weight γj∈[0,1]\gamma_j \in [0, 1]γj​∈[0,1]:

h(j)=s−[(1−γj) 2jDH−2  +  γj]h(j) = s^{-\left[(1-\gamma_j)\,\dfrac{2j}{D_H-2}\;+\;\gamma_j\right]} h(j)=s−[(1−γj​)DH​−22j​+γj​]

The two extremes of γj\gamma_jγj​ recover the methods already studied:

γj\gamma_jγj​ h(j)h(j)h(j) Behavior
000 s−2j/(DH−2)s^{-2j/(D_H-2)}s−2j/(DH​−2) NTK-aware
111 s−1s^{-1}s−1 Full PI

For j=0j=0j=0 with γj=0\gamma_j=0γj​=0: h(0)=s0=1h(0) = s^0 = 1h(0)=s0=1, so the highest-frequency dimension is always left unchanged regardless of the choice of γj\gamma_jγj​.

6.2 NTK-by-Parts: Frequency Segmentation

The wavelength of dimension pair jjj is:

Tj=2πωj=2π θ2j/DHT_j = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_j} = 2\pi\,\theta^{2j/D_H} Tj​=ωj​2π​=2πθ2j/DH​

YaRN uses two empirically motivated thresholds based on how many full rotations each dimension completes within [0,S−1][0, S-1][0,S−1].

High-frequency boundary jhj_hjh​. Dimensions completing more than 32 full rotations, i.e., S/Tj>32S/T_j > 32S/Tj​>32:

Tjh=S32  ⟹  2π θ2jh/DH=S32  ⟹  jh=DH2log⁡θ ⁣S64πT_{j_h} = \frac{S}{32} \;\Longrightarrow\; 2\pi\,\theta^{2j_h/D_H} = \frac{S}{32} \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{j_h = \frac{D_H}{2}\log_\theta\!\frac{S}{64\pi}} Tjh​​=32S​⟹2πθ2jh​/DH​=32S​⟹jh​=2DH​​logθ​64πS​​

Low-frequency boundary jlj_ljl​. Dimensions that do not complete even one full rotation, i.e., S/Tj<1S/T_j < 1S/Tj​<1:

Tjl=S  ⟹  2π θ2jl/DH=S  ⟹  jl=DH2log⁡θ ⁣S2πT_{j_l} = S \;\Longrightarrow\; 2\pi\,\theta^{2j_l/D_H} = S \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{j_l = \frac{D_H}{2}\log_\theta\!\frac{S}{2\pi}} Tjl​​=S⟹2πθ2jl​/DH​=S⟹jl​=2DH​​logθ​2πS​​

The three bands and their assignments:

Band Range γj\gamma_jγj​ Effective h(j)h(j)h(j) Rationale

For the transition band, γj\gamma_jγj​ ramps linearly from 0 (NTK-like at j=jhj=j_hj=jh​) to 1 (PI-like at j=jlj=j_lj=jl​), producing a smooth interpolation with no discontinuities.

6.3 Temperature Compensation

Applying any form of position scaling subtly distorts the attention distribution. Compressed position differences (m−n)/s(m - n)/s(m−n)/s reduce the rotation angle entering Rn−m\mathbf{R}_{n-m}Rn−m​, which shrinks the expected variance of the inner product qmTRn−mkn\mathbf{q}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{n-m} \mathbf{k}_nqmT​Rn−m​kn​. A lower variance yields a flatter softmax distribution — equivalent to raising the softmax temperature — which increases perplexity.

YaRN compensates by multiplying the pre-softmax score by a constant t>1t > 1t>1:

Amn=t⋅qmTRn−m kn\mathbf{A}_{mn} = t \cdot \mathbf{q}_m^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{R}_{n-m}\, \mathbf{k}_n Amn​=t⋅qmT​Rn−m​kn​

This lowers the effective softmax temperature, sharpening the attention distribution back toward its training-time shape. The YaRN paper reports the calibration t≈0.1ln⁡s+1t \approx 0.1\ln s + 1t≈0.1lns+1.

7. Summary

Each method in this progression diagnoses a more refined failure mode and fixes it while disturbing as little of the remaining behavior as possible:

Method Position transform Problem solved Remaining issue
Sinusoidal PE m↦mm \mapsto mm↦m (additive) Absolute position awareness No relative distance encoding
RoPE m↦Rmm \mapsto \mathbf{R}_mm↦Rm​ (rotary) Exact relative distance Fixed context length SSS
Naive extrapolation mmm unchanged beyond SSS — Low-freq OOD at large mmm
PI m↦m/sm \mapsto m/sm↦m/s (uniform) Low-freq OOD eliminated High-freq resolution loss
NTK-aware θ↦αθ\theta \mapsto \alpha\thetaθ↦αθ Both extremes handled smoothly Mid-range not independently tuned
YaRN Piecewise h(j)h(j)h(j) + temperature ttt Per-band optimal treatment + variance correction Minor fine-tuning cost

The thread running through every step is a single observation: different frequency components of RoPE behave fundamentally differently under context extension. High-frequency dimensions are robust to extrapolation; low-frequency ones are not. Every method from PI onward is, at its core, an increasingly precise attempt to act on this asymmetry — until YaRN, which addresses it dimension-by-dimension.
